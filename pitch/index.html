Hazardous Employment

A darkly comedic fantasy guild-management roguelite where the heroes fight monsters and you fight payroll.

30-second pitch

Management decisions create combat outcomes.

Recruit adventurers, choose contracts, set formations, assign equipment, and send teams into automated expeditions. There is no direct combat control and no last-second rescue. The consequences come back to the guild as injuries, deaths, morale problems, payroll obligations, debt, and occasionally useful heirlooms.

“The player wins or loses before the battle starts — through staffing, spending, and risk tolerance.”
Core loop

Prepare. Observe. Manage the fallout.

01

Recruit & prepare

Hire classes with different potential and wages. Match the party, formation, and equipment to the contract.

02

Observe the expedition

Combat resolves automatically from the decisions made before departure.

03

Manage the consequences

Balance loot, injuries, morale, debt, payroll, and a business that is always one bad week from disaster.

Positioning

Familiar strategy ingredients, combined around a different fantasy.

Known ingredients

Party composition, contract selection, scarcity, permanent consequences, and roguelite progression are already readable to strategy players.

The combination

Guild management + hands-off autobattles + business failure pressure + persistent employees + dry HR comedy.

Pitch takeaway

Hazardous Employment is not about clicking faster than the enemy. It is about making the staffing decision you can still afford after everything goes wrong.

Differentiation

Why this game instead of another autobattler?

Hands-off combat

Preparation matters more than reflexes; expeditions play out automatically.

Business failure pressure

Payroll, debt, and liability turn fantasy casualties into management problems.

Persistent employees

Survivors gain history and value, while injuries and deaths carry economic weight.

Dry HR comedy

The fantasy adventure is filtered through bureaucracy, waivers, staffing, and doomed paperwork.

Funding

Seeking finishing capital, not prototype capital.

The game is already playable and public. Funding is intended to raise presentation quality, increase testing, protect the release schedule, and finish the commercial version without expanding scope.

Working target: approximately $20K–$35K depending on partner scope and how much art, marketing support, and runway are included.
